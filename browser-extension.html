Q:How do we rescue RFx teams from dozens of clunky, inconsistent vendor portals and give them something that's actually a pleasure to use?

A:Loopio Browser Extension

The goal of this project was to replace dozens of different painful portal experiences with one consistent interface, and let AI do the scanning so a job that used to take hours of copy-pasting takes minutes. It also doubled as the first live test of our new brand.

Role
Lead product designer
Timeline
6 months to v1
18 months of iteration since
Team
1 PM · 5 engineers · design system team (multiple designers)
Tools
Figma · Claude Cowork · Claude Design · Gong · Google Forms
Problem Definition

Portal work was manual, repetitive, and easy to get wrong

A lot of RFx work happens inside vendor "portals" - a custom procurement sites where an RFP gets posted. The only way in is to log in, read every question, and copy it out somewhere you can actually work. This time is wasted in setup and for teams juggling dozens of live RFPs, it could eat the first hour of a session before the first answer is written.

Customers told us this plainly, call after call:

“The SmartScan feature in the portal was so, so much easier than uploading a document to Loopio or having to manually copy and paste questions to a project.”Proposal Content Manager, a healthcare data and analytics company
“It's great to see the portal import process being made streamlined and made more automated.”Proposals team member, a benefits administration technology company

We didn't need a better copy-paste workaround. We needed the portal itself to become an entry point into Loopio.

This is what teams were actually working in: a real procurement portal (Ariba, on the left).

Research Methods

An internal alpha, then a customer beta

I ran this as a research program, not a feature ticket. We started with an internal alpha with our sales and customer success teams since they talk to customers about these portals every day. I was able to get fast, well-informed feedback before running a full external cycle. From there we moved into a wider customer beta, then general release.

AI in the process

Claude Cowork took my interview notes, survey responses, and months of call transcripts and found themes in a fraction of the time a manual pass would have taken. Claude Design helped me push through wireframe directions fast, especially the hardest problem here: fitting a dense RFx question and a confident answer into a few hundred pixels of sidebar. Work that used to take half a day, wireframing especially, dropped to about 30 minutes.

Design System

A new brand, applied for the first time

This project doubled as the company's first external test of a brand refresh: new type, new palette, a different visual language for the product. I worked with others on the design team to help define what that system would actually look like applied to a real, shipped surface, under real constraints.

BeforeThe old Quick Scan flow: teal header, purple accents, pre-refresh type
AfterLoopio brand refresh: Forest, Cream, Teal, DM Serif Display / Albert Sans
Final Design

How it works

The shipped result points the extension at a portal question, click Scan, and it lands inside Loopio as a structured project. Every question is parsed out, matched against your content library, ready to answer or auto-fill. No more losing track of which answer belonged to which question, no reformatting, no second document open on the side.

Results

What happened after launch

Usage in the first three months after launch tells a clear story: this isn't a light, everyday tool. It's a batch tool, hit hardest in short, high-pressure windows. One global insurance brokerage ran 187 scans in a single day this July. A regional health insurer ran 137 in one sitting in August. Across just the seven accounts shown here, that's over 1,000 scans run in concentrated bursts, right when RFx teams needed the most relief.

Total scans per account in the first three months since launch, with peak single-day bursts called out.

“SmartFill and SmartScan work well together, and it's fantastic, and that's what really saves us time.”A real estate and property management software company
“What I like most about the browser extension is the speed and efficiency of filling in content. Capturing 90–95% accuracy, it picks up the majority of the work that needs to be done.”A laboratory information systems company serving healthcare and public-health labs
In a training session with an insurance provider, one proposal coordinator's live reaction said as much as any survey could: she wanted to know, immediately, whether this could pull in the platform-based RFPs her team was already buried in.

I led this initiative end-to-end, from the first customer conversation to a shipped, adopted feature: directing a PM and an engineering team, running the research myself, and helping define how the company's new brand would show up in a real product. Eighteen months on, it's still getting used most when it matters most: during RFP season.
